# Sindhudurg Fort (Malvan): buildings, built features and path network inside the walls

Conventions used throughout. "Published" = stated in a cited text. "Derived" = my own calculation from OpenStreetMap (OSM) geometry or from Esri World Imagery, so it is measured on a map, not on site. "Estimate" = reasoned inference, uncited. Local metre frame used for all coordinates: origin = OSM main-gate node [2713509503](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/2713509503) (16.043824 N, 73.461471 E); x = East, y = North; 106,984 m per degree longitude, 110,574 m per degree latitude. Bearings are compass bearings (0 = N, 90 = E). Source-quality warning: the web-search and WebFetch tools returned AI-summarised page text, so single-blog statements about interior layout are weak; the two Gazetteers and the OSM geometry are the strongest material.

## Shivrajeshwar temple (plan, size, roof, entrance, approach)

### Takeaway
The only measured figure found is the revised Gazetteer's "45 feet in length and 23 feet in breadth" (13.7 x 7.0 m) for the Shivaji temple, which is less than half the 28 x 10 m in the current model. The "timber-pillared hall" appears to come from a different temple (Girye Rameshwar) and is unsupported for Shivrajeshwar; the sabhamandap (assembly hall) is a later addition by Shahu of Kolhapur.

### Cited Findings
- Published size: "The temple of Shivaji ... 45 feet in length and 23 feet in breadth" = 13.7 x 7.0 m; built under Rajaram (reign 1689-1700). Image is black stone, head covered with a silver or gold mask. — [Revised Maharashtra State Gazetteer, Malvan entry](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- 1880 Gazetteer: the fort was "once full of buildings" and is now "a mere shell with nothing inside but a few small" temples (Bhavani, Mahadeo, Jarimai, Mahapurush, Shivaji); a solitary baobab (gorakh chinch) stands near the temple; 1862 list: 13 houses, 3 temples, 1 rest-house. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880, Ratnagiri places (Malvan)](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)
- 1880 Gazetteer temple allowance Rs 1,522 (1812 figure). — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)
- Built 1695 by Rajaram, described as being at the "centre of the fort"; idol seated in warrior pose, beardless, sailor's cap, covered by mask and garments; a roughly 4 ft (1.2 m) sword is displayed (another source says 3.5 ft, 1.07 m). — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- The assembly hall (sabhamandap) was built by Chhatrapati Shahu of Kolhapur; the temple holds a seated Shivaji statue (stated to be unique to this fort) and silver hand and foot imprints plus a sheathed sword. — [Tripoto trip report](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa)
- A visitor describes the temple as simple, surrounded by trees. — [Inditales](https://inditales.com/sindhudurg-fort-konkan-coast)
- Walk order from the gate: gate, paved path, Jarimari shrine, Shivrajeshwar (centre of fort), Mahadev behind it, wells, palace ruins. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- The "20 carved timber pillars, tiled roof" hall belongs to Shri Dev Rameshwar at Girye (1780), a different Sindhudurg-district temple; it was not found in any Sindhudurg Fort source. — [Wikipedia: Shri Dev Rameshwar Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Dev_Rameshwar_Temple)
- Candidate footprints (derived): OSM way 426199502 is a 13.9 x 8.6 m rectangle (area 120 m2, long axis bearing 85 deg, i.e. almost E-W) centred (-182,-116); its corners are (-189.8,-112.5) (-189.0,-121.1) (-175.2,-119.8) (-176.0,-111.2). Way 426199509 is 12.8 x 7.7 m (98 m2, long axis 90 deg) centred (-190,-100). — [OSM way 426199502](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199502), [OSM way 426199509](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199509)
- Imagery (derived, own visual read): the cluster around (-215..-172, -107..-125) is surrounded by a teal-tinted paved courtyard; a bright cream, flat-roofed building (about 18 x 9 m) sits at about x -205..-187, y -114..-123, directly west of way 426199502, and is not mapped in OSM; brown clay-tile gable roofs match ways 426199502, 426199509, 426199524. — [Esri World Imagery](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export)

### Inferences
- Scale the temple to about 13.7 x 7.0 m of main hall (published) rather than 28 x 10 m. OSM way 426199502 (13.9 x 8.6 m) matches the published length within 2 percent and is my best candidate for the Shivrajeshwar footprint; the cream building and way 426199509 are alternates. This identification is inference only; no source labels any OSM footprint.
- Treat the later sabhamandap as a lower, wider, plastered hall on the long side or one end of the sanctum; there is no source for pillars, so do not add a carved timber colonnade. Roof form (tile gable vs low dome vs flat) is not documented; the tile-roofed neighbours in imagery argue for a tiled gable roof, with low confidence.
- The long axis of the candidate footprints runs E-W, so the entrance is most plausibly at an east or west short end, or on a long side facing the paved court; Maharashtra temples commonly face east but I found no source for this temple, so verify against photographs.
- The model's 28 m length would not fit: the whole OSM cluster including its courtyard is only about 43 x 18 m, and the gap between ways 426199502 and 426199524 is 3.0 m.

### Gaps
- Entrance direction, plinth height, roof form, shikhara or deepmal, compound wall: not found in any fetched text. UNESCO nomination dossier (Maratha Military Landscapes, 2025) was blocked (HTTP 403 on whc.unesco.org) and not read.
- No source states which OSM footprint is the temple.
- The baobab's exact position ("near the temple") is not located.

## Other shrines: Mahadev, Jarimari, Maruti/Hanuman, Bhavani, Mahapurush, footprint shrines

### Takeaway
Shrines are small; only Shivrajeshwar has a published size. The Maruti/Hanuman shrine is at or just inside the gate, the Jarimari shrine is near the entrance (small, inscription of 1881), and Mahadev with a square stepwell/kund is behind or next to the Shivrajeshwar temple. Sources disagree on Jarimari and Mahadev placement.

### Cited Findings
- Revised Gazetteer: shrines of Bhavani, Mahadeo, Jarimai, Mahapurush and Shivaji; "a temple of Maruti near the gate". — [Revised Gazetteer, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Small domed Hanuman shrine with a stone idol "in front of the gate"; brick Nagarkhana (drum house) above the gate; two domed shrines on the ramparts near the entrance mark Shivaji's left foot and right hand imprints. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Imprints are to the right of the entrance. — [oocities trekforts](https://www.oocities.org/trekforts/f_sindhudurg.html)
- A footprint shrine is a small temple-like structure on the bastion to the right of the gate. — [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa)
- Marathi Wikipedia: gate faces east, followed by a north-facing gap and then an umbar/teak door; a small Maruti temple follows after the door. — [Marathi Wikipedia: Sindhudurg](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- Jarimari: small temple along the paved path after the gate with an inscription dated 1881. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); located "in front of the main entrance" — [oocities](https://www.oocities.org/trekforts/f_sindhudurg.html); contradicted by the Marathi Wikipedia, which places Jarimari on the west side among the residents — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- Mahadev temple has a square deep stepwell, a Nandi and a linga, and sits behind Shivrajeshwar — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); contradicted in placement by "next to" — [oocities](https://www.oocities.org/trekforts/f_sindhudurg.html) and "further along from" Shivrajeshwar, with a Shivaji-era underground passage — [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa).
- Mahapurush temple: listed after the palace ruins and quarry reservoir, no detail. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Bhavani: listed among the shrines, no location or founding date. — [Revised Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- A Ganesh carving exists on one bastion wall (not a freestanding shrine). — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Gate area (derived): OSM way 426199519 is a 12.4 x 6.1 m rectangle with long axis N-S (bearing 178 deg), corners (-10.6,1.8) (-10.1,-10.6) (-4.0,-10.4) (-4.5,2.0), i.e. just inside (west of) the gate node; the main track passes within 1.2 m of its north side. — [OSM way 426199519](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199519)
- Imagery (derived): a round bastion stands about (+32,-38), just south-east of the gate; the sea face of the east wall runs south-south-west from it. — [Esri World Imagery](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export)
- The 1880 text lists only small temples as the remaining contents, and the 1862 list counted 3 temples. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)

### Inferences
- Sizes are not published; the tiny OSM footprints (way 426199521 at (-177,-103), 6.6 x 5.2 m, 34 m2; way 426199500 at (-211,-201), 5.7 x 4.9 m, 28 m2) are plausible shrine-sized candidates, but no source ties them to any shrine.
- Place the Hanuman/Maruti shrine at or inside the gate, a few metres either side of the track start at (0,0); keep it a 2-3 m domed or flat-roofed plinth shrine rather than a hall.
- Place Mahadev north of or immediately adjacent to the Shivrajeshwar footprint (candidates: way 426199509 or way 426199521), with the square stepped kund beside it, because "behind", "next to" and "further along" can all be satisfied by a spot 5-10 m from the main temple; do not put it on the track.
- Jarimari: put it next to the main track between the gate and the temple court (most sources) rather than "on the west among residents" (one source); a 3 x 3 m shrine fits within the track's first 60 m.

### Gaps
- Dimensions, doors and orientations of every shrine except Shivrajeshwar; Bhavani and Mahapurush locations; Ganesh or other deepmal positions. None found.
- Which OSM or imagery footprint is each shrine: unsourced.

## Wells, tanks and kund

### Takeaway
Sources give three freshwater wells (Dudh, Dahi, Sakhar) each in a square stone structure, but the revised Gazetteer says "four wells and two tanks"; none has a published diameter, depth or position, and OSM has no well or pond features inside the fort.

### Cited Findings
- "Four wells and two tanks of drinking water." — [Revised Gazetteer, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Three freshwater wells (Doodhbav, Dahibav, Sakharbav), each surrounded by a square stone structure, located beyond the temples; a quarry reservoir (the construction quarry flooded for water) is a further source; Mahadev has a separate square stepwell. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Three stone wells; a dry pond is used for vegetables and washing. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- "Three sweet water wells"; sweetness explained either as natural or as rainwater harvesting. — [Inditales](https://inditales.com/sindhudurg-fort-konkan-coast)
- A rainwater pond lies near the Daryaburuj bastion, west of the palace ruins. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Imagery (derived, own visual read): a low, green, wet-looking vegetated patch lies at about x -190..-150, y -45..+30 beside the north-west wall; a horseshoe-shaped open enclosure of about 23 x 11 m (OSM way 426199498) sits at (-230,-349) on the south coast. These are visual candidates for a pond and a tank respectively, unverified. — [Esri World Imagery](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export), [OSM way 426199498](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199498)

### Inferences
- Model three square wells of about 2-3 m inside side parapets near the temple cluster and palace ruins (SW of the temples, along the lane south of (-190,-150)); do not exceed that without a source. Diameters and depths are estimates only; Konkan laterite wells are commonly 2-4 m across, which is general knowledge and not cited.
- Reconcile "four wells" with "three": the Mahadev stepwell likely accounts for the fourth.

### Gaps
- Diameter, depth, parapet height, steps and exact positions of Dudh, Dahi and Sakhar Bav; the "two tanks" are not identified. No source with plans was reachable.

## Residences, community, school and shops

### Takeaway
Evidence supports about 13 houses (1862) and about 15 families today, not 24. Mapped OSM footprints number 20 including temples and the gate zone, with a median area of roughly 107 m2 for the 19 non-gate footprints; the buildings are tile-roofed, with some sheet-roofed and a few flat-roofed structures.

### Cited Findings
- 1862 list: 31 acres, 13 houses, 3 temples, 1 rest-house (the same Gazetteer also gives the area as 48 acres). — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)
- 1828: the west and south walls were in ruins and the buildings were wretched except the magazine and gateway; huts of the Gabit community stood among the coconut palms. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)
- About 15 families live inside, mostly descendants of mavlas; younger generation moved ashore; tourism and fishing are the livelihoods; houses are old and cannot be sold. — [Lakshmi Sharath](https://lakshmisharath.com/inside-story-sindhudurg)
- Residents grow coconut and vegetables. — [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa)
- A small primary school exists; a pre-primary school is also mentioned. — [Revised Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa)
- Small shops sell drinks and snacks. — [Inditales](https://inditales.com/sindhudurg-fort-konkan-coast)
- Footprint statistics (derived from 20 OSM building ways; see the full table in the last section): 19 non-gate footprints range from 17 to 221 m2 with a median of about 107 m2; typical house about 9-14 m x 6-10 m; areas of 17 to 52 m2 are sheds or shrines. — [OSM building ways 426199498 to 426199525](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199502)
- Imagery (derived): brown clay-tile gables dominate; red/pink tile roofs at ways 426199522 (-230,-124) and 426199525 (-191,-304); small blue sheet-roofed buildings at about (-192,-241), (-156,-325), (-202,-345) are not in OSM; a cream flat-roofed building at about (-196,-118) is not in OSM. — [Esri World Imagery](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export)

### Inferences
- Use about 12-15 houses, not 24; of the 20 mapped footprints, one (way 426199519) is a gatehouse zone, one or two are temples, and about 4 are tiny sheds or shrines.
- Three groups: residential lane along the track between y -145 and -200 (ways 426199504, 426199511, 426199513, 426199500); an outlying west group around (-246,-152), (-239,-97), (-230,-124) (ways 426199518, 426199520, 426199522); outliers at (-210,-53), (-128,-71), (-191,-304), (-134,-385).
- Single-storey, with an eaves height of about 2.5-3 m, a Mangalore-tile or country-tile double-pitch roof and a front verandah are general Konkan conventions I could not source; label as assumptions.
- Door orientation guidance: see the last section; houses with long axes N-S along the lane should have doors on the E (lane) side if west of the lane and on the W side if east of it.

### Gaps
- Heights, materials and plans of the houses; the school's location; shop locations. None found.
- The claim of "15-20 families" is not sourced beyond a guide's "about 15".

## Ruins: palace, granaries, magazines, barracks, lime works

### Takeaway
There is a palace complex in ruins beyond the wells (west side), the Daryaburuj/Nishan bastion with a rainwater pond is west of it, and lime pits and a grinding stone are near the Rani chi Vela landing. No dimensions, no plans.

### Cited Findings
- Palace complex ruins lie beyond the wells; the Daryaburuj / Nishankathi bastion is a tall, isolated bastion west of the palace ruins, and a 28-foot (8.5 m) saffron flag flew there until 1812; a rainwater pond is near it. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Lime pits and a large grinding stone remain near the landing; a sealed sea gate is reached by the parapet and a bastion chamber; Chilkhati bastion is double-walled. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- 30-40 latrines are built into the walls. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- Secret rooms in the walls and a 5 km tunnel are asserted; the 5 km tunnel is explicitly a local claim, not established. — [oocities](https://www.oocities.org/trekforts/f_sindhudurg.html); [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- 1828: the magazine and gateway were the only non-wretched buildings. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html)
- Walls 29-30 ft (8.8-9.1 m) high and about 12 ft (3.7 m) thick, about 32 towers and narrow staircases up the wall; bastion counts of 52 or 53 and 45 staircases appear elsewhere. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html); [Revised Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/) (conflict: 32 towers vs 52 bastions)
- Imagery (derived, own visual read): a brown, eroded, terraced linear feature at about x -290..-260, y -125..-55, west of the residential cluster, could be quarry cuts or collapsed foundations; a round bastion stands at about (-234,-44) on the north wall; OSM way 426199526 (barrier=city_wall, 634 m2) is a wall arc from (-218,-30) to (-185,-1). — [Esri World Imagery](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export), [OSM way 426199526](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199526)
- OSM way 426199496 (barrier=wall), about 11 x 15 m, lies at about (-239,-308). — [OSM way 426199496](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199496)

### Inferences
- Put the palace ruin as low foundation walls and rubble (not a standing building) in the west-south-west part, around x -300..-250, y -125..-55, with the Nishan bastion on the nearby west wall; confidence low and unverified.
- Do not model granaries, barracks or magazines as standing structures: the 1880 text says nothing but a few small temples remained.

### Gaps
- No ruin plans, dimensions, or names beyond "palace complex"; no sourced location of granaries, barracks, magazine, or "Rani cha Mahal".

## Path network (gate, temple lane, wall access, circuit)

### Takeaway
OSM maps one 432 m main track from the gate that runs diagonally south-west to the temple cluster (215 m of path, 207 m in a straight line), turns south down the lane of houses, and continues south to the southern wall; two further tracks branch west and south. The published walk follows gate, paved path, Jarimari, Shivrajeshwar, Mahadev, wells, ruins, then a rampart walk back to the gate.

### Cited Findings
- Entrance at the NE corner (1880); entrance faces east and is hidden, then a north-facing gap, then the door. — [Bombay Gazetteer 1880](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Ratnagiri%20and%20Sawantwadi/ratnagiri/places1_m.html); [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- Rampart walk circuit; cannon positions along the walkway near the entrance; walk continues round the ramparts back to the main gate. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- 45 staircases to the walls (revised Gazetteer; "45 stairways" in durgbharari) and narrow staircases up the walls (1880). — [Revised Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- A paved path leads from the gate. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- OSM main track (highway=track, tracktype grade2, way 426199529): vertices (0,0) (-2,3) (-13,3) (-18,-5) (-20,-12) (-24,-17) (-33,-24) (-88,-60) (-100,-69) (-144,-101) (-171,-116) (-178,-125) (-183,-138) (-189,-149) (-193,-164) (-195,-181) (-195,-191) (-190,-226) (-190,-235) (-192,-243) (-198,-255) (-208,-276) (-210,-281) (-209,-284) (-209,-288) (-206,-294) (-200,-299) (-183,-309); chainage: 25 m at the first bend, 116 m at (-88,-60), 215 m at the temple court (-171,-116), 254 m at (-189,-149), 296 m at (-195,-191), 412 m at (-200,-299), total 432 m. — [OSM way 426199529](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199529)

### Inferences
- Track widths are not published; assume 2-3 m for the paved/dirt lane and 1-1.5 m for wall-top walkways (estimate). The main track is digitised loosely (about 1-3 m), so building edges that touch it are probably a digitisation artefact, not real encroachment.
- Gate to temple is a long, straight, diagonal approach of about 200 m across open ground with palms and scrub, not a short bazaar street.

### Gaps
- No published widths, surface, or step counts for individual stairs; the wall-top walkway is not mapped in OSM; tourist circuit direction beyond the blog account is not documented.

## Rani chi Vel

### Takeaway
Rani chi Vela is a crescent-shaped landing reached through a small gate in the wall, further along the wall from the Daryaburuj bastion, below the southern rampart. It is not mapped in OSM.

### Cited Findings
- A small gate in the wall opens onto the crescent-shaped landing called Rani Chi Vela, further along the wall from the bastion near the rainwater pond; lime pits and a grinding stone are near it. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Rani chi Vela is below the southern rampart. — [Tripoto](https://www.tripoto.com/trip/sindhudurga-fort-unexplored-history-5ff95ab3e89fa)
- OSM tags only one beach, way 1425300057 at about (75,87) (near the gate, outside), not Rani chi Vel. — [OSM way 1425300057](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1425300057)

### Inferences
- The south coast near the end of OSM track 426199528 at (-215,-348) is a plausible access point (the track ends at the shore beside way 426199498); this is a candidate, not a finding.

### Gaps
- Exact position, beach dimensions and gate size: not found.

## Spatial relationships, OSM footprints and placement checks
