# Natural topography and coastal setting of Sindhudurg Fort (Kurte island, off Malvan)

Scope: Sindhudurg Fort only (Kurte island, Malvan, Sindhudurg district, Maharashtra). Local frame assumed by the model: metres, +X east, +Y north, +Z up, Z = 0 at mean sea level (MSL), origin about 16.04253 N, 73.45908 E. Evidence class is tagged where it matters: MEASURED (instrument or survey), NOMINAL (published round figure), QUALITATIVE (description). Feet are converted to metres (1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 fathom = 1.83 m, 1 mile = 1.61 km).

Shorthand for repeated sources: "1880 Gazetteer" = Gazetteer of the Bombay Presidency Vol. X, Ratnagiri and Savantvadi (OCR text of the full volume), [archive.org wbsl.19227](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt). "Kulkarni 2026" = M. V. Kulkarni, PhD thesis "An Armoured Chain: An Architectural Analysis of the Maratha Sea Forts of Konkan", University of Auckland, [ResearchSpace](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966), Ch. 6 (Sindhudurg). "Tripati 1997" = Tripati and Gaur, IJNA 26.1:51-57, [PDF](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf).

## (a) Island bedrock and landform: natural high and low points inside the walls

### Takeaway
No published spot height, "highest point", knoll or hollow exists for Kurte island in anything found; the only landform statements are qualitative ("low island", "rocky", "wave-worn rock and stretches of sand"). The bedrock is contested between sources (19th-century Kaladgi quartzite vs. modern "basalt/Deccan Trap" generalisations vs. UNESCO's "dykes"), so no single rock type can be asserted. The only quantitative hint of relief is the 30 m SRTM/ASTER pattern (higher centre-south, lower north strip), which is canopy- and building-biased (see question f).

### Cited Findings
- The 1880 Gazetteer describes Sindhudurg as standing "on a low island about a mile from the shore" (QUALITATIVE). — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- 1880 Gazetteer, geology section: "At Mélvan, a small patch of these Kaladgi beds juts into the sea, and the Vengurla rocks, Sindhudurg, Kavda rock, and other smaller rocks off the coast, also consist of the same hard beds" (Kaladgi series = Precambrian quartzite and shale). This is a 19th-century field statement, not a modern survey. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- The Gazetteer's Malvan sub-division text describes the mainland around Malvan as laterite plateaus, and Rajkot (the mainland fort) as standing on rising ground surrounded on three sides by sea. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); Kulkarni 2026 likewise says Rajkot sits "on a natural elevation" and is flanked by sea on three sides — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Conflicting rock-type claim: Kulkarni 2026 states (as a general statement about Konkan sea forts, not Sindhudurg-specific) that nearby local stone was basalt / Deccan Trap — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966). The 2017 field guide says Konkan shore platforms are "mainly produced on basalt", with lateritic platforms in places, and that the laterite cap is about 8-12 m thick (thinning south of Tarele) — [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf). These regional statements conflict with the Gazetteer's Kaladgi claim for the Sindhudurg rock itself.
- The UNESCO nomination dossier caption (doc 221274) says Kurte island "also provided the raw material for construction" and "The Dykes (volcanic stones) are still observed on fort" (QUALITATIVE; implies igneous dykes, not Kaladgi sediments). — [UNESCO document 221274](https://whc.unesco.org/en/documents/221274)
- Marathi site durgbharari.in describes the island as rocky and says the fort's reservoir was formed from a stone quarry on the island, i.e. building stone was cut on the island. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Tripati 1997: the anchors cemented in the fort jetty are of "locally available porous, fine laterite and sandstone" (grey sandstone, yellow sandstone, laterite); this indicates sandstone and laterite are both locally available on the Malvan coast, but it does not say what the island bedrock is. — [Tripati 1997](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf)
- Footnote 9 of the 1880 Gazetteer (quoting South Konkan Forts): the plan is "highly irregular with many projecting points and deep indentations", so that every point outside the rampart is commanded from inside. This describes the wall trace, not relief. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- The Malvan coast generally is described as "dominated by rocky outcrops with intermittent sandy beach" (NCCR Malvan page; seen only as a search-result snippet, page not fetched, so treat as unverified). — [NCCR Malvan PDF](https://nccr.gov.in/sites/default/files/Malvan.PDF)

### Inferences
- The island is best modelled as a low, irregular rocky platform with a gentle internal relief, not as a hill. The wall is recorded as low and sea-washed on the west and south (see b, c), which is only consistent with an island whose interior stands at most a few metres above sea level at the margins.
- Because three sources point to three different rock types, a modelling decision on surface material should rely on the texture of the standing masonry (reported dark/black stone, lime-mortared) rather than on bedrock geology. Treat the Kaladgi claim as the best-dated primary statement but not confirmed.
- The DEM high in the centre-south (see f) could be real ground (a mild mound around the central temple and well cluster) or vegetation/structure bias; there is no independent ground-truth, so any knoll should be low-amplitude (a few metres at most) and flagged as uncertain.

### Gaps
- No spot heights, contours, "highest point" statements, knolls or marsh/hollow descriptions for Kurte island were found. The dried tank/lake inside the fort (see g) is the only recorded depression.
- An aggregator page (Grokipedia) calling Kurte a "coral island" and the walls "locally quarried laterite" was seen and rejected as low-quality and internally inconsistent; it is not used anywhere in these notes.
- No geological map or section specific to the island (GSI sheet, Sukhtankar and Pathani 1991 M.Phil. thesis "Coastal geological studies around Malvan" at [Shivaji University repository](https://ir.unishivaji.ac.in/handle/123456789/2615) was identified but not accessed).
- The UNESCO nomination dossier site plans and management plan (whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739) were not read in full; they may contain a topographic survey.
- An OpenStreetMap "natural" feature query (Overpass) timed out (HTTP 504), so OSM-mapped rock/sand polygons were not checked.

## (b) Interior ground height above sea level; relation of interior to wall foot

### Takeaway
No measured interior ground elevation was found. Qualitative evidence says the island is low and the sea-facing walls are so low-set that at one place they seem almost below high-water level, with wave-worn rock and sand inside; waves still enter on the west at high tide. So the wall is not a retaining wall holding up a raised interior on the west/south: interior and exterior ground are roughly level there, about at or slightly above high-tide level.

### Cited Findings
- Gazetteer footnote 10 (quoting Nairne's MS): "on the sea side so low are the walls that at one place they seem almost below high water level, and inside of the fort are masses of wave-worn rock and stretches of sand" (QUALITATIVE; the OCR is slightly garbled around "sea side"). — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- Kulkarni 2026: "The irregular shape of the low-lying island gives the fortified wall an irregular appearance"; "In some parts on its west end, the fortification is so low that it allows the high waves to enter inside the fort during a high tide"; in the 1900s part of the west wall was damaged forming a breach approximately 6 m wide (wording "6 metres within" is ambiguous). — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- 1828 survey (Gazetteer footnote 11, Southern Konkan Forts): the north and east faces were in very fair repair; the west and south faces were "deplorable": parapet washed away by the monsoon, often "destroyed clear away from the level of the ground", the terreplein (cannon platform) washed away "leaving great blocks of rough stone", and large stretches of the west wall and smaller parts of the south wall were undermined. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- Wall dimensions (NOMINAL, ASI entrance board, per Kulkarni 2026): height about 9.1 m, thickness about 3.7 m — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966). The Gazetteer gives 29-30 ft (about 8.8-9.1 m) high, on average 12 ft (about 3.7 m) thick, and "about thirty-two towers from forty to 180 yards [about 37-165 m] apart" — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt). English Wikipedia repeats 30 ft / 12 ft — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhudurg_Fort). Bastion count conflicts: 32 (Gazetteer), 42-52 (Kulkarni 2026), 52 (durgbharari.in), 52 vs 22 (Marathi Wikipedia).
- Area: 48 acres (about 19.4 ha) per the Gazetteer (the 1862 civil-forts list gave 31 acres, about 12.5 ha) and Kulkarni 2026 (about 19 ha, perimeter about 3 km); the Gazetteer says "little less than two miles" (about 3.2 km) round the ramparts. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Wall access: about 45 flights of stairs from ground to ramparts; entrance at the north-east corner behind a large bastion that projects further into the sea than the others and conceals the entry passage. — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Interior in 1880: "a mere shell with nothing inside but a few small [buildings/huts]", a temple, numerous coconut palms and a solitary baobab; 1862 list: no garrison, water abundant. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- Marathi Wikipedia infobox gives "200 feet" as a height; the figure is unexplained and inconsistent with all wall data, so it is not used. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97)
- Footing: molten lead was poured into the foundation to resist saline water (popular figures: over 4,000 lb of lead per English Wikipedia, about 500 khandi per Marathi Wikipedia; these are unverified popular numbers). — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhudurg_Fort)

### Inferences
- Along the west and south walls the inside ground is plausibly within about 1-2.5 m of MSL, i.e. at or just above spring high water (see d for tide levels), since waves overtop the low sections and the 1828 survey found the terreplein gone down to rough boulders. A starting model value of about +1.5 to +2.5 m MSL at the west/south wall foot, interior and exterior alike, is defensible but unverified.
- North and east faces were "in fair repair" in 1828, consistent with a more sheltered and possibly more built-up ground there; the DEM shows the north strip at about 2-4 m (SRTM) and 3-6 m (ASTER) (see f). The current model guess of 2.6-4.65 m interior is within that DEM range and not contradicted by anything found.
- The wall is therefore best modelled as a free-standing sea wall founded on the rock level, with the interior ground approximately level with the exterior rock foot; the rampart is reached by about 45 stair flights, consistent with a wall that stands well above the interior ground as well. There is no evidence of a raised terrace inside.

### Gaps
- No measured interior ground level, trench/excavation levels, or ASI survey datum was found. The ASI board and the UNESCO dossier drawings may carry levels but were not accessed.
- Whether the interior ground sits above or below the exterior rock at the NE gate and north/east faces is not documented.
- The garbled OCR of footnote 10 leaves some doubt over exactly which stretch is "almost below high water level".

## (c) Exterior toe and foundation: rock shelves, reefs, boulders, sand; beaches (NE gate beach, Rani chi Vel)

### Takeaway
The fort stands on a rocky, reef-fringed island: the sea-facing toe is rock and boulder on the west and south (wave-attacked), with a small jetty platform at the NE entrance and a small crescent beach (Rani chi Vela) reached through a postern in the south rampart. No dimensions, slopes, or intertidal-platform widths were measured for this island.

### Cited Findings
- Kulkarni 2026: a postern gate in the south rampart was a secret doorway; "The bank of the island that can be accessed through this side is called Ranicha Vela", described in the figure caption as a "private beach", believed to be where Queen Tararani privately swam and sailed. This places Rani chi Vela on the south side. — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- durgbharari.in calls Rani Chi Vela a crescent-shaped landing reached by a small gate; it also mentions a small sea gate (now sealed) for direct sea access and lime-processing pits near the landing. It does not give the side or dimensions. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- A Marathi web-search summary said Rani chi Vel is "a small crescent-shaped soft-sand beach under the south wall"; this is consistent with Kulkarni 2026 but was not seen on a fetched page. Dimensions and slope are not given anywhere.
- Marathi Wikipedia: the main entrance faces east and the access gap faces north; the sea lies to the west and south. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97)
- Tripati 1997: "A platform at the fort entrance serves as a jetty"; stone anchors are cemented on it and used as mooring stones; the bay between Malvan jetty and Sindhudurg is "very shallow and rocky and navigable only by small canoes" and submerged rocks near the fort may have caused shipwrecks. — [Tripati 1997](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf)
- Kulkarni 2026: ferries approached "by helical turns around the rocky bay" because of "irregular rocks on and beneath the water surface"; the fort's entrance is "intricate and constricted"; surrounding waters are rocky and obstructed by reefs. — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Wall-foot condition (see b): west and south faces undermined and washed in 1828, with "great blocks of rough stone" where the terreplein was — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt). Kulkarni 2026 describes thick lime pointing and lead in the foundations at the base against saline waves — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966).
- Regional shore-platform analogues (not island-specific): Konkan shore platforms average widths below about 30 m, ending in a low-tide cliff of about 1 m, pocked by pools and potholes; Sarjekot cliffs "appear to have been left high and dry due to recent fall in sea level". — [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf)
- Malvan beach (Malvan-Devbag) is 1.7 km long in the field guide's measured table (Table 6) — [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf); this is the mainland beach, not the island.

### Inferences
- A reasonable exterior-toe model: rock shelf and boulders at about 0 to +1 m MSL around the west and south walls (intertidal, under 30 m wide by the regional analogue), a small sand pocket at Rani chi Vela on the south, and a built jetty/landing platform at the NE gate. Widths for the model should be treated as plausible, not measured.
- Because the 1828 survey records west/south wall undermining and waves entering at the west, those faces should carry visibly more rubble/boulder at the foot than the sheltered north/east.

### Gaps
- No width, slope, sediment type, or area for Rani chi Vela, nor for any beach at the NE gate and jetty; no measured intertidal platform width at Kurte.
- No site-specific description of exposed reef/rock shelves around the north and east sides.
- The exact position of the jetty relative to the NE bastion is not documented in sources found.

## (d) Tidal data at Malvan: range, MHWS/MLWS relative to MSL, exposure, chart datum vs MSL

### Takeaway
Malvan is micro-tidal, mixed semi-diurnal, with a spring range somewhere between about 1.5-1.9 m (published and field-measured) and about 2.4 m (satellite-model forecast); no official Survey of India/Admiralty constants for Malvan were found. A working assumption of MHWS about +1.0 m and MLWS about -1.0 m relative to MSL (range 1.9-2.4 m) is defensible, with ±0.2 m uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Field guide Table 4 (source: Naval Hydrographic Report on the West Coast of India, 1981): Malvan (16°03′N, 73°28′E) spring-tide height 1.5 m (NOMINAL; the neap column in the table is garbled and could not be matched to Malvan). — [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf)
- Field guide Table 6 (author-measured): Malvan tidal range TR(S) 1.9 m and TR(N) 1.3 m (MEASURED; S and N presumably spring and neap or two sectors, labels not defined in the extract). The same guide says range rises from 1.5 m at Redi to 5.4 m at Valsad, defines micro-tidal as spring range under 2.0 m, and says tidal currents on the south Konkan coast rarely exceed 10 cm/s. — [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf)
- 2024 Journal of Coastal Research abstract on the Malvan-Devbag beach: micro-tidal (0-2 m), semi-diurnal; longshore currents 0.2-1.0 m/s. — [JCR 2024](https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-coastal-research/volume-113/issue-sp1/JCR-SI113-048.1/Morpho-Dynamics-of-Malvan-Devbag-Beach-Maharashtra-West-Coast-of/10.2112/JCR-SI113-048.1.full)
- Satellite-model tide forecast for Malvan (16.07 N, 73.47 E), datum "Mean Lower Low Water (Satellite)": 30-day extremes +2.37 m and -0.08 m (about 2.45 m extreme range); an example day shows 2.22, 0.99, 2.06 and 0.24 m. These are model predictions, not gauge records. — [tide-forecast.com Malvan](https://www.tide-forecast.com/locations/Malvan/tides/latest)
- Tides Atlas Malvan (16.06 N, 73.463 E): 10-day heights 0.55-2.45 m, datum not stated, so not usable as MSL-referenced values. — [Tides Atlas Malvan](https://tidesatlas.com/en/india/maharashtra/malvan)
- Conflicting regional statement: Kulkarni 2026 (citing Nairne) says the Konkan tidal range increases from 3 m to 5 m from the south to the north end — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966); this is higher than the field guide's 1.5 m (Redi) to 5.4 m (Valsad) and Malvan's 1.3-1.9 m, and is a generalisation, not a Malvan figure.
- Access and exposure: island forts cannot be accessed at high tide, and the Konkan island forts are closed from about June to September for monsoon sea conditions (rough sea, reduced visibility, submerged intertidal access); English Wikipedia attributes the monsoon closure to high tides. — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhudurg_Fort)

### Inferences
- From the tide-forecast extremes (+2.37 / -0.08 m above its MLLW datum), mean sea level is about 1.1-1.2 m above that datum, giving MHWS about +1.2 m and MLWS about -1.2 m relative to MSL (spring range about 2.4 m). From the measured 1.9 m range, MHWS is about +0.95 m. The two disagree by about 0.25 m at HW; the 2.4 m forecast likely includes the satellite model's coarse-grid bias and the 30-day maximum (not a typical spring). A single working value of +1.0 m (HW) / -1.0 m (LW), with the monsoon surge/swell adding transient run-up, avoids false precision.
- If Indian chart datum is close to the forecast's MLLW datum (unverified), a charted depth D lies about D + 1.0-1.2 m below MSL; for the modelling frame (Z = 0 at MSL), chart depths therefore need that offset added before use.

### Gaps
- No official Survey of India or Admiralty (secondary-port) tide constants for Malvan; no gauge record; HAT/LAT, MHWS/MLWS and the MSL-to-chart-datum offset are inferred, not published.
- The field guide's Table 6 labels "TR(S)" and "TR(N)" were not defined in the extract.
- The nearest standard port with a long gauge record is probably Mormugao (Marmagao, Goa; PSMSL/GLOSS), but no published offset from it to Malvan was found, and its PDF could not be parsed.
- No wave-height or swell-exposure data specific to the fort; only qualitative statements (west and south faces storm-battered; monsoon closure).

## (e) Bathymetry near the island and the channel to Malvan; Padmagad, Rajkot, Sarjekot and other rocks

### Takeaway
The channel between Kurte and the Malvan shore is shallow, rock- and reef-strewn, and described at roughly 1-1.5 km wide (with older accounts as small as a quarter mile); safe anchorage for larger ships is only 8 fathoms (about 14.6 m) abreast the fort. Padmagad is a tiny half-reef half-sand island close to the shore that is walkable at low tide. No gridded bathymetry, soundings, or reliable bearings/distances to Padmagad, Rajkot, Sarjekot or Dandi were found.

### Cited Findings
- Distance from the island to the mainland (conflicting): about 1 km off Malvan (Tripati 1997) — [Tripati 1997](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf), 1 km — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); 1.5 km — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966); "about a mile" (about 1.6 km) — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); "half a mile" (about 0.8 km) — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97); and the Gazetteer's Malvan bay text puts the two outer islands "about a quarter of a mile" (about 0.4 km) from the shore, which conflicts with its own "about a mile" figure.

Abreast the fort a large ship should not anchor in less than eight fathoms [about 14.6 m]"; the small steamer Johnston Castle was wrecked in 1865 on a sunken rock now marked by a buoy a quarter of a mile (about 400 m) from the north end of Sindhudurg island; landing is best in the little bay north of Malvan point with a south wind and in Malvan harbour with a north-west wind. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- Kulkarni 2026: Malvan was never a favourable harbour because of rocky waters and reefs; access to Malvan bay is only through "a narrow, rock-lined channel", which prevents large ships from reaching Sindhudurg and Padmagad (citing Nairne, 1894). — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Padmagad: 1880 Gazetteer, "about half a mile from the mainland and within a mile of Malvan", "half reef half sand-bank, with ruins and cocoanut palms", about 1 acre (about 0.4 ha); at low tide connected to the mainland by a neck of sand. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt). Kulkarni 2026: Padmagad is 1 km from the Malvan shore, 1 acre, shallow concealed rocks make sailing there difficult, and it can be approached by walking at low tide — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966). Tripati 1997 also says Padmagad is walkable at low tide — [Tripati 1997](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf).
- Rajkot: the old island of "Medha", now joined to the mainland (the channel dried up); Rajkot fort lies on rising ground at the headland sheltering Malvan bay, in the town to the north of Sindhudurg. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- Sarjekot (conflicting distances): the Gazetteer puts it at the creek mouth "about a mile and a half" (about 2.4 km) north of Malvan; Kulkarni 2026 says about 4 km north of Malvan at the Talashil river mouth, but also about 3 km north of Rajkot, and (citing Nairne) a river 3.2 km from Malvan. — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- The Vengurla rocks lighthouse is about five miles (about 8 km) south of Malvan — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt).
- Modern dive records: rocky reef at 7-15 m depth in the Malvan marine sanctuary (Old Lighthouse dive site; not fort-specific); WII 2023 habitat mapping reports 28% coral cover across the sanctuary (Kawda rock, Seven Rocks, lighthouse area). — [Dive Against Debris](https://diveagainstdebris.org/debris-data/old-lighthouse-424609); [WII repository](https://digitalrepository.wii.gov.in/items/6f5f5aec-76d2-4a01-9a44-5eaa338aac4f)
- Gridded bathymetry check: GEBCO 2020 (15 arc-second) returned between -8 and +2 m at six sample points on and around the island, i.e. too coarse to resolve it (a +2 m value on the island and -8 m just offshore indicates pixel averaging). EMODnet 2018 returned null. — [OpenTopoData gebco2020](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/gebco2020)

### Inferences
- Most modern sources (1 km, 1.5 km) cluster at 1.0-1.5 km from the island to the Malvan shore, so a modelling channel width of that order is consistent with them; the Gazetteer's 0.4 km and the Marathi Wikipedia 0.8 km figures may refer to the nearest edge of the reef flat rather than the open channel, or be slips. This reading is inferred.
- The rock that wrecked the Johnston Castle in 1865 lay about 400 m north of the island's north end, which suggests a shallow rock field extending north and north-east toward the entrance channel; the model should avoid a clean deep-water approach on that side.
- Large ships needed 8 fathoms (about 14.6 m, tide state unspecified) abreast the fort, which implies deep water exists offshore; in the rocky bay between the island and Malvan the floor is likely much shallower (the bay is "navigable only by small canoes"). Any sea-floor model there is inferred from the descriptions, not measured.
- The Rajkot, Padmagad and Sarjekot positions relative to the fort can be computed from published coordinates once a reliable coordinate source is chosen; the sources found here give only rough distances.

### Gaps
- No bathymetric soundings, chart (e.g. Indian Naval Hydrographic Office chart) values, or multibeam data for Malvan bay were found; the Admiralty India Pilot and Survey of India charts were not accessed.
- No Dandi reef data were found; no bearings from the fort to Padmagad, Rajkot, Sarjekot or the Johnston Castle rock are published in the sources found. The Sarjekot Wikipedia page was judged unreliable (wrong coordinates).
- The Gazetteer's own distances conflict (a quarter mile vs about a mile) and no source resolves which is the channel width.

## (f) DEM data at useful resolution

### Takeaway
SRTM 1 arc-second (30 m) and ASTER GDEM 30 m both resolve the island as only about 17 x 25 cells and read 1-11 m with a consistent "high in the centre-south, low in the north" pattern; both are surface models and are almost certainly biased high by coconut palms and structures. GEBCO is unusable. Better ground-truth would need drone photogrammetry or LiDAR, none of which was found publicly.

### Cited Findings
- Method: researcher-queried the OpenTopoData public API (nearest-neighbour at native posts) on a 1 arc-second grid, lat 16°02'48" down to 16°02'18" N and lon 73°27'22" to 73°27'45" E (31 rows x 24 columns), on 2026-10-13. — [OpenTopoData srtm30m](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm30m); [OpenTopoData aster30m](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/aster30m)
- Footprint: 304 SRTM land cells (about 27.7 ha at about 911 m² per cell, versus about 19 ha enclosed by the wall, i.e. the DEM includes the fringe and mixed sea/land cells); 223 ASTER land cells (the ASTER grid is offset/shifted relative to SRTM). Land = value above 0; open sea reads 0. — [OpenTopoData srtm30m](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm30m); [OpenTopoData aster30m](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/aster30m)

### Inferences
- For a plausible ground surface, treat SRTM/ASTER only as relative shape: the lowest quartile of interior cells (2-4 m) is the best DEM-based upper bound for bare ground; values above about 6 m are likely canopy/building-biased by several metres. A ground model of about 2.0-5.0 m MSL with a gentle central-south rise (about +1 to +2 m over the mean) is more defensible than reproducing 8-11 m. The model's current 2.6-4.65 m guess lies inside this envelope.
- The SRTM/ASTER high ridge (centre-south) lies over the area of the central Shivrajeshwar temple and well cluster (per durgbharari.in, "middle of the fort"), so a coincident canopy/structure bias is plausible; palms are reported fort-wide, not specifically there (not verified).
- Datum (general knowledge, not sourced in this research): SRTM and ASTER GDEM heights are referenced to an EGM geoid, approximately MSL, so they can be read as MSL heights, subject to the surface biases above.
- Datasets to try next (names only, none retrieved): Copernicus GLO-30, ALOS AW3D30, CartoDEM, and above all a drone photogrammetric DSM or LiDAR survey. The 30 m products would add little over SRTM for a 500 m island; only drone/LiDAR can give sub-metre ground truth.

### Gaps
- Copernicus GLO-30, ALOS AW3D30 and CartoDEM values were not retrieved (rasterio/GDAL were not available in the environment and no tile download was done); no public LiDAR or drone DEM of Kurte island was found.
- The API's nearest-neighbour sampling hides interpolation and sub-pixel registration shifts between the two DEMs; the ASTER grid appears shifted relative to SRTM.
- No DEM void/quality mask was checked.

## (g) Drainage, the wells (Dudh Bav, Dahi Bav, Sakhar Bav) and the water table

### Takeaway
Three freshwater stepped wells (Dudh, Dahi, Sakhar baav) sit in the central part of the fort, plus a fourth well inside the Mahadev temple, a dry lake/tank and a quarry-formed reservoir; the 1862 civil-forts list called the water supply abundant. No depths, water-table levels or drainage layout were found anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Three potable-water wells named Dahibaav (curd), Sakharbaav (sugar) and Dudhbaav (milk); "baav" means stepped well; they are built of dressed local stone with lime mortar; water was used by the garrison and offered to temple deities. The Gazetteer (1962 State reprint, p. 774, as quoted) says there were originally four wells and two tanks; a fourth well sits inside the Mahadev temple (with a rumoured tunnel); there is "evidence of a dried lake in the fort, fed from the same water sources as the wells". — [Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)
- durgbharari.in: the three wells lie in the middle of the fort surrounded by square stone structures; the water stays sweet although the surrounding sea is salt; a reservoir was made from a stone quarry; a rainwater pond lies near the Daryaburuj bastion; a square, deep stepwell is at the Mahadev temple behind the Shivrajeshwar temple. — [durgbharari.in](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Marathi Wikipedia: three sweet-water wells; a dry tank used to grow vegetables in the monsoon. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97)
- The 1862 civil forts list: water at Sindhudurg was "abundant" with no garrison; Padmagad's water supply was "defective"; the Gazetteer adds that Malvan town's wells near the sea are "a little brackish". — [1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt)
- Inditales (travel blog; unverified) doubts natural wells because the seabed is rocky, favours a rainwater-harvesting explanation, and says monsoon water lasts the year. — [Inditales](https://inditales.com/sindhudurg-fort-konkan-coast)

### Inferences
- Fresh water on a small low island that is overtopped by high-tide waves is almost certainly a thin rainwater lens floating on sea-water, with the well water level very near sea level (within about 1-2 m of MSL, tidally influenced). This is hydrogeological reasoning, not a measurement, and is consistent with Malvan's near-sea wells being "a little brackish".
- A model should therefore show the wells as stepped shafts whose water surface lies only slightly above MSL, so the stair descent is roughly the local ground height minus about 1 m; depth and step counts are not published, so this is a placeholder.
- The "dried lake" and quarry-reservoir indicate a shallow hollow in the interior that fills in the monsoon; its location is not given beyond "in the fort", with a rainwater pond near the Daryaburuj bastion on the west.

### Gaps
- No published depths, shaft dimensions, step counts, or water-table levels for any of the wells; no drainage-channel or outlet details.
- No water-quality or salinity measurements (only the qualitative "sweet" claim and the Malvan brackish-well note).
- The relative position of the quarry/reservoir, dry tank, and the Daryaburuj pond within the fort is not mapped in sources found.
