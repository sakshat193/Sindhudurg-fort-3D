# Sindhudurg Fort: wall circuit, bastions and gate geometry

Conventions used below: metres first, original unit in parentheses. Reliability tags: MEASURED (surveyed or instrument figure; none found), NOMINAL (round figure from a gazetteer, ASI board or official text, basis unstated), ANECDOTAL (tourism or blog text, unverifiable). 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 yd = 0.9144 m, 1 mile = 1.609 km, 1 acre = 0.4047 ha.

## How tall is the curtain wall, and how does height vary around the circuit?

### Takeaway
Every source converges on a NOMINAL wall height of about 8.8-9.1 m (29-30 ft), with about 10 m as the high alternative, and the basis (toe, interior ground or foundation) is never stated. The only non-uniform evidence is qualitative: one sea-face stretch looked almost at high-water level, the west end is low enough for waves to enter at high tide, and in 1828 the west and south faces had lost most of their parapet and platform while the north and east faces were in fair repair. No source gives a segment-by-segment height table.

### Cited Findings
- The Maharashtra Gazetteers Department text for Malvan says "The walls are low, ranging from twenty-nine to thirty feet" (8.84-9.14 m), with a circuit "a little less than two miles round the ramparts" (under 3.22 km). NOMINAL. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); the older text (32 towers) is reproduced at [shivshahi.tripod.com](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindgaze.html)
- Footnote citing "Nairne's MS": "On the sea side so low are the walls that at one place they seem almost below high water level, and inside of the fort are masses of wave-worn rock and stretches of sand." The location is not named. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [shivshahi.tripod.com](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindgaze.html)
- 1828 survey (footnote): north and east faces "in very fair repair", with a few small fig trees. West and south faces "deplorable": parapet nowhere entire, mostly washed away "so as to leave not above two feet" (0.61 m) in places, in many parts destroyed to ground level, and the whole terreplein (cannon platform) washed away leaving "great blocks of rough stones". A large stretch of the west wall and smaller parts of the south wall were undermined, and it was doubtful the west wall would stand many more years. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- 1862 list of civil forts: walls and bastions "with few exceptions in fair order", so repairs had been made after 1828. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Auckland thesis (Kulkarni 2026, ch. 6): the wall is "neither massive nor lofty", comparatively low, and at some parts of its west end so low that high tides let waves enter the fort. It gives about 9.1 m high and 3.7 m thick, cited to the ASI entrance board (NOMINAL). — [Kulkarni thesis, Univ. of Auckland](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- 30 ft (9.1 m) high and 12 ft (3.7 m) thick: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhudurg_Fort); [Maharashtra Tourism](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/sindhudurg/) (30 ft high, 12 ft thick). Both are NOMINAL and echo the gazetteer.
- Higher or lower alternatives: about 10 m, per [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/) and [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/sindhudurg-the-martaha-fort-that-cemented-shivaji-maharajs-naval-dominance-9549833/); "rampart rises to 10 m", per [indianmilitaryhistory.org](https://indianmilitaryhistory.org/forts/sindhudurg.html); 9 m, per [Bharat Expedition](http://www.bharatexpedition.com/2013/01/forts-of-india-sindhudurg-fort.html) and CCRT as summarised in [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/).
- ANECDOTAL: "The height of side walls were raised depending on the strength of the wind." — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- ANECDOTAL: the gate bastions are said to be very different heights (see the main gate section for the 20 ft and 40 ft figures). — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)

### Inferences
- "Ranging from 29 to 30 feet" implies a near-constant height over most of the circuit. A single 8.8-9.1 m default is consistent with the nominal sources, and the variation lies in a few named exceptions (the sea-face low spot, the west end, the damaged W and S faces).
- The 1828 W/S damage removed the parapet and terreplein, not necessarily the whole wall. It shows exposure to monsoon swell, not a lower design height. Whether the "almost below high water" stretch is original design or erosion is not stated.
- The exposed-vs-sheltered pattern is clear: N and E (harbour and Malvan side) were intact in 1828, W and S (open sea) were ruinous. If modelling present condition, W/S crest and parapet should be lower or broken. If modelling the original, keep the nominal height everywhere.
- The nominal figure is probably an outer-face observation (the gazetteer remark on the "sea side" reads like a view from outside), but this is unverified. The interior ground is natural "wave-worn rock" and sand, so wall height above interior ground is likely less than toe-to-crest at those places.
- The user's traced circuit of 2,989 m (1.86 miles) fits "a little less than two miles", so the traced plan is consistent with the gazetteer circuit.

### Gaps
- No per-segment height data (no table, section drawing or survey) in any accessible source. The UNESCO nomination dossier and ICOMOS evaluation (site 1739) returned HTTP 403 (bot challenge) and were not read; their figures are unknown beyond the second-hand ICOMOS remarks in Anantam IAS.
- The datum for "29-30 ft" (outer toe, interior ground or foundation) is never stated.
- The location of the "almost below high water" stretch (Nairne's MS) is not given.
- No photo-based height estimates were made. No LiDAR or DEM-based wall measurement was found.
- Height near the beach (Rani chi Vela) and near the gate: no data.

## How thick is the wall, and what is its cross-section (batter, walkway, parapet, merlons, loopholes, drainage, foundation)?

### Takeaway
The only quantitative thickness is the NOMINAL 12 ft (3.66 m) "on an average", with 3 m, 4 m (foundation) and 8-10 ft (2.4-3.0 m) alternatives, and an attributed Shivaji letter saying width varies by place. Nothing sourced gives batter angle, walkway width, parapet or merlon dimensions, loophole sizes, spout details or course heights.

### Cited Findings
- Gazetteer: walls "on an average twelve feet thick" (3.66 m). NOMINAL. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- ASI board via thesis: 3.7 m thick. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- 3 m wide, 9 m high, 4 km zigzag wall, 42 bastions: [Bharat Expedition](http://www.bharatexpedition.com/2013/01/forts-of-india-sindhudurg-fort.html); [RTF case study](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/case-studies/a2791-sindhudurg-fort-the-1664-architecture-marvel-constructed-on-an-island/). Secondary sources, likely rounded.
- Foundation walls "4 m thick laid in lead", rampart rises to 10 m, about 3.2 km walls. Secondary. — [indianmilitaryhistory.org](https://indianmilitaryhistory.org/forts/sindhudurg.html)
- Attributed Shivaji letter to Hiroji: the foundation must be broad; where bare rock is below, a massive structure above; leave two hath of space on each side and raise the wall of that width; the wall is wider in some places and narrower in others. Paraphrase of a quoted letter; the exact wording is ambiguous. — [Darya Firasti](https://daryafirasti.com/2020/04/15/msindhudurg/); the Indian Express also mentions a letter on solid-rock foundations with sufficient width: [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/sindhudurg-the-martaha-fort-that-cemented-shivaji-maharajs-naval-dominance-9549833/)
- Foundation technique: slots cut for foundation stones and molten lead poured to fix them, per the state tourism text and Vishwakosh as summarised in [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/); molten lead in foundations and lime pointing at the base of sea-exposed walls, per the [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download).
- Lead quantity: "500 khandi" in the west and south wall foundations, per [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग); "over 40,000 pounds of lead" tagged [citation needed], per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhudurg_Fort). ANECDOTAL; the two figures were not reconciled.
- Battered walls (thick at the bottom, thin above) are described as general Maratha practice in the thesis's Vijaydurg discussion, not as a measured Sindhudurg figure. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- Parapet and top: the towers have embrasures for cannon, "a flat seat on the parapet" in most, and stones projecting inwards drilled with flagstaff holes. The 1828 text calls the rampart top a "terreplein or cannon platform". — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Loopholes (jangya) are present in the bastions. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- The outer wall is zigzag and "constructed in such a way as to facilitate the attack on the enemy" (flanking fire). The 1828 note says the plan has "many projecting points and deep indentations" so that no outside point is out of fire from somewhere inside. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- ANECDOTAL: the zigzag defence wall is "between eight & ten feet in width" (2.4-3.0 m); three mighty defence walls were built parallel to the foundation against waves; the inner floor slopes toward the defence wall. — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)

### Inferences
- 3.66 m is an average. Shivaji's letter and the 8-10 ft figure suggest real variation, plausibly from about 2.4 m to 4 m or more, wider where the wall rests on bare rock or faces the open sea. This is an inference, not a measurement.
- 12 ft on average with a 4 m foundation hints at a modest batter or offset, but no source states one. For Blender, a battered profile (for example outer face inclined, inner face near vertical) is a modelling assumption, not documented geometry.
- The terreplein and "flat seat on the parapet" suggest a continuous walkway or gun platform behind the parapet, wide enough for guns on bastions. Width unknown.
- An interior floor that slopes toward the wall (ANECDOTAL) would imply drainage through the wall, but no spouts are documented.

### Gaps
- Batter angle, base-vs-top thickness, walkway width, parapet height and thickness, merlon (charya) and embrasure (fanji) sizes, loophole size and spacing, drainage spouts, course heights and block sizes: no source found.
- The khandi-to-kg conversion and the 500 khandi vs 40,000 lb reconciliation were not sourced.
- The ICOMOS and UNESCO dossier section drawings were not accessible (HTTP 403).

## Bastions: how many, what shape, size, spacing and height, and which are special?

### Takeaway
Counts conflict widely (22, 32, 42, 52). The older gazetteer text says about 32 towers, the revised state gazetteer says about 52, and CCRT and Vishwakosh say 42. Shape is consistently outstanding semicircles with embrasures, spaced 40-130 yd (36.6-118.9 m) apart. The user's 42-terrace model matches CCRT and Vishwakosh, but the survey count itself is better evidence than any text.

### Cited Findings
- About 32 towers, 40 to 130 yards apart (36.6-118.9 m), "generally outstanding semi-circles with fine embrasures for cannon" — older gazetteer text at [shivshahi.tripod.com](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindgaze.html); also 32 towers in [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/).
- The Maharashtra Gazetteers Department version reads "about fifty-two towers from forty to 130 yards apart" and also adds 45 staircases and a 20 ft breach. This version is post-1960 (it says the allowance "has been continued by the Government of Maharashtra"). — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- 42 bastions: CCRT and Vishwakosh, per [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/) and [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); [Bharat Expedition](http://www.bharatexpedition.com/2013/01/forts-of-india-sindhudurg-fort.html).
- 52 bastions and 45 stairways: [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग), which also says "22 bastions" elsewhere on the same page (self-contradictory); [Kokan Bhatkanti](https://www.kokanbhatkanti.com/place/sindhudurg-fort) is inconsistent (52 and 42 both stated). The thesis says "between 42 and 52". — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- Half-circles offset the wall on the outside. Semicircular bastions are comparable to Janjira. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- A large bastion projects further into the sea than the others and conceals the entry passage. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- Bastion tops: "a flat seat on the parapet" in most, and flagstaff-hole stones projecting inwards. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Nishan (flag) bastion, also called Daryaburuj / Nishankathi Buruj: a tall isolated bastion west of the palace ruins, with a rainwater pond near it. A flag at 28 ft (8.5 m) is said to have flown until 1812. — [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/). It lies "inside the wall, past the wells". — [Darya Firasti](https://daryafirasti.com/2020/04/15/msindhudurg/). RTF describes it as central. — [RTF](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/case-studies/a2791-sindhudurg-fort-the-1664-architecture-marvel-constructed-on-an-island/)
- Flagstaff: "228 ft" (69.5 m) in [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग) and [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/) (the latter also lists a 50 ft / 15.2 m watch tower to the south), against 28 ft (8.5 m) in Durgbharari. ANECDOTAL and conflicting.
- Other named bastions: Chilkhati (double-walled), and a bastion wall with a Ganesh carving. — [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- Gate-flanking bastions of 20 ft (6.1 m) and 40 ft (12.2 m): ANECDOTAL. — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- Shivaji's rule: gates are placed so the approach falls under bastion fire. — [Darya Firasti](https://daryafirasti.com/2020/04/15/msindhudurg/)

### Inferences
- The "32 vs 52" jump is between the older text and the post-1960 state gazetteer. The 52 may count smaller projections and the 32 only large semicircular towers; this is speculation. 42 sits between them.
- Average spacing along a 2,989 m circuit would be about 93 m for 32, 71 m for 42, 57 m for 52 bastions, all inside the quoted 36.6-118.9 m range, so spacing cannot choose the count.
- The Nishan bastion is probably an interior tower, not part of the curtain circuit, so it should not be counted among the perimeter terraces.
- Bastion heights relative to the curtain are undocumented except for the anecdotal figures. Gate bastions may be asymmetric in height and the sea-projecting gate bastion is bigger than the rest.
- 228 ft for a flagstaff is likely an error or exaggeration; 28 ft (8.5 m) is plausible for a staff on a bastion. Inference only.

### Gaps
- No bastion-by-bastion table (diameter, projection, height above curtain) found. The gazetteer's 130 yd upper spacing and the semicircular form are the only geometric constants.
- The official ASI or CCRT list of bastion names and positions was not accessible.
- Diameters and projection distances are unsourced.

## What is the geometry of the main gate (Mahadarwaja) complex?

### Takeaway
The main gate is at the NE corner, hidden in a gomukhi arrangement: a boat sees a gap only when close, the passage runs behind a large sea-projecting bastion, and a timber door with a wicket (dindi darwaza) leads to an arched guard chowk, with a nagarkhana above and a Hanuman shrine just inside. Dimensions are anecdotal or absent.

### Cited Findings
- Entrance at the north-east corner. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html). "It was constructed in such a way that it could not be easily detected by the enemy"; a Maruti temple stands near the gate. Same source.
- The gate is at the NE where the sea is deep and the gate is hidden, with water even at low tide; a ruined nagarkhana over the Mahadarwaja. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/)
- The CCRT places the gate behind two bastions, and ICOMOS calls it a hidden semi-circular entrance. — [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- Thesis: a large bastion projects further into the sea than the others and conceals the entry, with the passage cutting behind it. The passage is narrow, slightly widening inside, with a gentle upward sloping path (not a spiral stair), recessed between two circular bastions. The main door is a large spiked timber door with a dindi darwaza, then an arched chowk for guards, round arches with lime-mortared voussoirs and impost, plus pointed arches. The nagarkhana above has semicircular, segmental and mahirapi arches. The door pivots top and bottom without hinges. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- Gomukhi gate, invisible until very close; a three-arched meghdambari above with devdya (guard rooms) inside; Hanuman faces south at the door. — [Darya Firasti](https://daryafirasti.com/2020/04/15/msindhudurg/); south-facing Hanuman also in [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/sindhudurg-the-martaha-fort-that-cemented-shivaji-maharajs-naval-dominance-9549833/)
- Main gate faces east and is approached through curved outer walls; a brick nagarkhana above the gate holds a broken cannon; a domed Hanuman shrine stands in front. — [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- After landing, a north-facing gap (khind) is seen; the door is of umber planks joined with teak; a small Maruti temple is inside with a path to the bastion from it. — [Marathi Wikipedia](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/सिंधुदुर्ग)
- ANECDOTAL: the door is "9 ft in height & 3.5 ft in width" (2.74 x 1.07 m); the nagarkhana was built by Shahu; the gate lies between two bastions of 20 ft and 40 ft (6.1 m and 12.2 m). — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- ANECDOTAL: the pathway admits only 4-5 people at a time. — [Bharat Expedition](http://www.bharatexpedition.com/2013/01/forts-of-india-sindhudurg-fort.html); [RTF](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/case-studies/a2791-sindhudurg-fort-the-1664-architecture-marvel-constructed-on-an-island/)
- Name: the main entrance is also called Dilli Darwaja. — [shivshahi.tripod.com](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindhu.html); [Districts Project](https://www.indiandistricts.in/cultures/maharashtra/sindhudurg/architecture/)
- Shivaji's footprint and handprint: two small niches on the rampart wall right of the main gate hold the left-foot and right-hand imprints, protected by small domes. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/) places the dome on the north rampart.

### Inferences
- The consistent picture is an indirect approach: boats come in under bastion fire, the opening faces a different direction from the sea approach (north-facing gap, east-facing gate), and the passage turns behind the large bastion. The exact turns are not documented, so layout must be taken from the survey and imagery.
- The 3.5 ft door width is too narrow for a main door; it may describe the wicket (dindi darwaza). Speculation. A "4-5 people at a time" passage suggests roughly 3-4 m width, equally speculative.
- The gate bastions are probably asymmetric (one larger, projecting further); the anecdotal 20/40 ft figures should be used only as ratios at most.

### Gaps
- Passage width and height, door and arch dimensions, courtyard size, guard-room sizes, step counts and risers: no measured source.
- Which of the two gate bastions is the larger, and the exact turn geometry, is not given.
- ASI plan drawings of the gate were not found.

## What secondary gates, sea gates and Rani chi Vel features exist?

### Takeaway
Sources agree on one small postern in the south or south-west rampart that opens onto the Rani chi Vela crescent beach, plus a possible sealed second sea gate. No source describes a broad opening or a lowered wall at the beach, only a small door.

### Cited Findings
- A crescent-shaped, soft-sand beach by the south wall is Ranichi Vela. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); "The dry area in south is known as Ranichi Vel", per [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- A postern gate (secret doorway) in the south rampart opens onto Ranicha Vela; the thesis says Sindhudurg has only concealed passageways. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- A small gate along the wall near the Nishankathi bastion leads to the crescent Rani Chi Vela; climbing the parapet leads to a bastion chamber and another hidden sea gate now sealed with stones. — [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)
- A small chor darwaja in a bastion on the west opens to a sand beach. — [Darya Firasti](https://daryafirasti.com/2020/04/15/msindhudurg/)
- Another entrance at Ranichi Vela to the beach. — [RTF](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/case-studies/a2791-sindhudurg-fort-the-1664-architecture-marvel-constructed-on-an-island/)
- A cross-wall inside with a secret boat escape. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/)
- A small wicket (dindi darwaza) exists in the main door. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)

### Inferences
- West and south descriptions probably refer to the same postern near the SW; the stronger sources (Vishwakosh, thesis, malvancity) say south. Treat as south or south-west.
- The beach is outside the wall, so Rani chi Vel is most likely a normal-height wall with a small door, not an opening. No evidence of a lowered wall there.

### Gaps
- Door sizes, exact positions and the sealed sea gate's location: not given.
- Whether the "almost below high water" wall stretch coincides with Rani chi Vela is unknown.

## What is the rampart access (staircases and ramps)?

### Takeaway
45 staircases is the commonly quoted count, but it appears only in the revised state gazetteer and sources copying it; the older gazetteer says only "here and there narrow staircases". No width or riser data exists.

### Cited Findings
- "Forty-five staircases lead from the inside to the top of the walls." — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- Older text: "Here and there narrow staircases lead from the inside to the top of the walls." — [shivshahi.tripod.com](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindgaze.html); narrow staircases also in [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- About 45 flights of stairs, with sources Ghanekar and the Maharashtra Gazetteers (1962). — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download); also [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/) and [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/sindhudurg-the-martaha-fort-that-cemented-shivaji-maharajs-naval-dominance-9549833/)
- Stairs descend from the rampart. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/)

### Inferences
- The 45 figure is likely a 20th-century count; stairs are "narrow" and internal, probably spaced between bastions. Locations are unknown.

### Gaps
- Stair locations, widths, riser and tread heights, and any ramps: no source.

## What about latrines, water outlets, towers and other wall furniture?

### Takeaway
Latrine counts are anecdotal (more than a dozen to 30-40). No sourced geometry for latrines, spouts or towers beyond the bastions.

### Cited Findings
- "More than a dozen water closets"; a dungeon on the west side. ANECDOTAL. — [malvancity.com](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/)

## What is the present condition: breaches, collapsed and restored sections?

### Takeaway
There is a documented west-wall breach (20 ft, 6.1 m, in the state gazetteer), localised damage, vegetation overgrowth and substantial ASI conservation. Wave damage on the west and south faces has been recorded since 1828.

### Cited Findings
- "The western side of the outer wall is now broken by dashing waves which have caused a breach of 20 feet in it." — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)
- The thesis says the west wall was damaged in the 1900s, forming a breach "approximately 6 metres within" (ambiguous wording); it also records vegetation overgrowth and dilapidation, 12-15 families, a school and food stalls inside. — [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)
- Some bastions and parts of the wall have collapsed from waves. — [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/)
- ICOMOS: localised damaged sections but substantial conservation; a steel lamp on the wall and buffer-zone buildings flagged as harmful (second-hand). — [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- 1828 and 1862 condition: see the first section.

### Inferences
- The thesis "6 metres" likely echoes the gazetteer's 20 ft (6.1 m); treat as one datum: a breach about 6 m wide in the west wall. Whether it is a gap width or a depth is ambiguous.

### Gaps
- The exact breach location, current restoration extents and vegetation extent: no survey data.
- The UNESCO and ICOMOS condition sections were not accessible.

## Which conflicting figures exist, and which are most reliable?

### Takeaway
No MEASURED dataset exists in the accessible sources. The most defensible references are the gazetteer figures (29-30 ft high, 12 ft average thickness, 40-130 yd bastion spacing, ~2 mile circuit) because they are traceable to named 19th-century observers, and the 1828/Nairne footnotes for the variation pattern. Tourism pages mostly copy these.

### Cited Findings
- Height: 29-30 ft (8.84-9.14 m) gazetteer; 9.1 m ASI board; 9 m CCRT/Bharat Expedition; about 10 m Vishwakosh/Indian Express. — see first section for links.
- Thickness: 12 ft (3.66 m) gazetteer average; 3.7 m ASI board; 3 m RTF; 4 m foundation (indianmilitaryhistory.org); 8-10 ft (malvancity). — see second section for links.
- Circuit: a little less than 2 miles (under 3.22 km) gazetteer; about 3.2 km, 3 km, 3.5 km Vishwakosh, 4 km CCRT/Indian Express/RTF. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/sindhudurg-fort/)
- Area: 48 acres (19.4 ha) gazetteer; 19 ha Vishwakosh; 20 ha Indian Express; about 15 ha indianmilitaryhistory.org; 31 acres (12.5 ha) in the 1862 list. — [Maharashtra Gazetteers, Malvan](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [Marathi Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); [indianmilitaryhistory.org](https://indianmilitaryhistory.org/forts/sindhudurg.html)
- Bastions: 22, 32, 42, 52 (see the bastions section). The indianmilitaryhistory.org "52" is called embrasures there, not bastions.

### Inferences
- Reliability ranking for modelling: (1) the user's own survey trace and imagery; (2) gazetteer text with its named footnotes (1828, Nairne, 1862); (3) ASI board and thesis (nominal); (4) CCRT and Vishwakosh summaries; (5) tourism and blog pages (anecdotal).
- The gazetteer's 2-mile circuit and the user's 2,989 m trace agree. The 4 km figures look like rounded or overstated values.
- Recommended modelling defaults, flagged as assumptions: height 8.8-9.1 m nominal with lowered, damaged W/S sea faces and one near-high-water stretch; thickness about 3.66 m average with local variation (wider on rock/seaward); 42 perimeter bastions with the gate bastion larger and the Nishan bastion treated as an interior tower; one NE gomukhi gate with a south or south-west postern at Rani chi Vela.

### Gaps
- UNESCO dossier and ICOMOS evaluation for site 1739 (whc.unesco.org) returned HTTP 403 (bot challenge) and were not bypassed; no ASI Mumbai Circle drawings found.
- No photo-based height estimates (people in frame) were done.
- The original 1880 Gazetteer volume text was seen only through reprints (state gazetteer web page, shivshahi.tripod.com, Anantam IAS); the precise 1880 wording of the 32 vs 52 towers should be checked against the printed volume.
