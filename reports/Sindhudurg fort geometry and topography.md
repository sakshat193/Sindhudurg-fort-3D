# Model Sindhudurg Fort From Sparse Evidence

Sindhudurg can be modelled as a **19.1 ha low rock island** (OSM coastline 2,799 m; traced wall circuit about 2.8 to 3.0 km) enclosed by a wall of nominal **9.1 m height and 3.66 m mean thickness**. The interior ground sits at about **+2.0 to +5.0 m above MSL** and holds a sparse village of **12 to 15 houses**, one small temple, a few shrines, three stepped wells and a single **432 m main track**. Nothing about the wall is measured: height, thickness, bastion count and gate geometry all rest on a gazetteer, an ASI board and a thesis, so each should be a swappable parameter rather than a baked constant. Four prototype assumptions should change. The constant wall height becomes a datum-explicit crest height plus a condition overlay. The 28 x 10 m timber-colonnaded temple shrinks to a **13.9 x 8.7 m** footprint with no colonnade. The 24 generic houses become **12 to 15** houses on mapped footprints. The 42 perimeter bastions stop including the Nishan bastion, which belongs in the interior near **(22 to 26, -205)**. The gate should also move to the OSM node at **(255.7, 143.1)**, 18 to 26 m from the position in the brief. The interior terrain should stay near the prototype's 2.6 to 4.65 m rather than adopt the 7 to 11 m the DEMs show, because those are surface models inflated by palms, roofs and wall tops. Use **1.65 m** as the adult male reference (eye height 1.53 m, camera 1.55 m) and a water plane at MSL moving +/-1.0 m with the tide. OSM, UNESCO and Wikimapia agree on position to about 10 m, which is the same order as the 0.9 to 1.2 m clearances that cause clipping, so the build needs a snapping and clearance pass rather than trust in any single dataset.

## The island is a low rock shelf, and the DEMs overstate it

Kurte island lies about **1.0 to 1.5 km** off the Malvan shore (sources range from 0.4 to 1.6 km), with roughly 8 fathoms (14.6 m) of water abreast the fort, the Johnston Castle rock about 400 m north of the island's north end, and Padmagad, a one-acre islet walkable at low tide ([Tripati 1997](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Sindhudurg-Tripati1997.pdf); [Kulkarni thesis](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/bitstreams/9039c261-fd32-486f-b5ac-d8deadde8d12/download)). The OSM islet coastline (way 19804981, 291 nodes) encloses **19.10 ha** over a perimeter of 2,799 m and spans X -221 to 301 and Y -398 to 352 in the model frame ([OSM way 19804981](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/19804981)). The UNESCO core polygon (19.1 ha) and the Wikimapia fort polygon (18.97 ha, 192 vertices, area centroid at **(76.6, -34.1)**) match it to within about 9 m, and the gazetteer's 48 acres (19.4 ha) agrees ([Maharashtra Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html)). The wall therefore follows the shoreline almost everywhere, and the single-point fort coordinates (OSM node at (108.0, 28.7), Wikidata at (83.3, -59.0), UNESCO at (68.7, -3.3)) are 8 to 257 m apart and should not be used for placement. Bedrock is contested (Kaladgi quartzite in the 1880 Gazetteer, basalt in the field guide and thesis, volcanic dykes in a UNESCO document) and a quarry exists on the island, so use a neutral rock surface ([1880 Gazetteer](https://archive.org/download/wbsl.19227/19227_djvu.txt); [Field guide](https://vanashakti.org/storage/resource/geomorphological-field-guide-book-on-konkan-coast-1700217267288.pdf)).

No spot height of the interior exists in any source I found; the 1880 Gazetteer simply calls it a "low island". The DEMs inside the wall's bounding box are what remain. SRTM 30 m gives 292 cells with mean 4.95 m, median 4 m and p10 to p90 of 2 to 8 m; ASTER gives mean 5.44 m; both peak at **7 to 11 m** in the centre-south, and the Copernicus GLO-30 maximum is 11.4 m at the south end ([OpenTopoData SRTM](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/srtm30m); [OpenTopoData ASTER](https://api.opentopodata.org/v1/aster30m)). At the model origin the products read 6.6 to 9 m, and at the OSM gate 1.0 to 5.3 m. These are surface models: palms, roofs and 9 m wall tops raise the cell values, a 30 m cell is wider than the wall, the sea is flattened to 0 (Mapzen reads 3 to 4 m over water), and products differ by 0.5 to 2 m in mean absolute terms. My recommendation, at **medium-low confidence**, is a smooth terrain of **2.0 to 5.0 m MSL** that keeps the prototype's 2.6 to 4.65 m interior as its core, adds a lower **1.5 to 2.5 m** foot along the W and S walls (the sea-battered sides), and raises a +1 to 2 m rise in the centre-south to about 4.5 to 5.0 m. The gate side should sit at the low end of the range, which is an inference from the gentle gate slope in the gazetteer, not a measurement. Do not import raw DEM heights above about 6 m.

Beyond the wall, the toe is a rock shelf narrower than 30 m with a sand pocket at **Rani chi Vela** (Wikimapia centre (-123.3, -193.4), 37.2 x 16.1 m) and a jetty platform at the NE ([Kulkarni 2026](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/items/bb42bae3-d326-4ad3-89b3-d5c5ed5b4966)). OSM also maps 15 submerged seamark rocks and bare-rock and beach polygons east of the fort, which can seed breaking-water hazards. Bathymetry is unusable (GEBCO and ETOPO do not resolve it), so any seabed slope beyond the 30 m shelf is invention; fade the shelf into an opaque deep-water plane instead. Wells draw on a freshwater lens whose level I infer to be near MSL, which puts water roughly 2.5 to 4.5 m below ground in the stepped wells.

The tide is micro-tidal and semi-diurnal. Sources disagree on range: the field guide's measured Table 6 gives 1.9 m (south) and 1.3 m (north), a nominal table gives 1.5 m spring, and tide-forecast sites give a mean of about 1.6 m, spring up to 2.6 m and neap 0.7 m ([tide-forecast.com Malvan](https://www.tide-forecast.com/locations/Malvan/tides/latest)). Use **MHWS +1.0 m and MLWS -1.0 m about MSL (+/-0.2 m)** as the working range, and note that chart datum lies about 1.0 to 1.2 m below MSL, so charted depths need that shift. The wall foot is genuinely wet on the exposed sides: Nairne described one sea-side stretch as almost below high-water level, and the thesis reports waves entering at the west end at high tide. Keep the water plane at Z = 0, bury wall bases at least 2 m below it to avoid visible seams, use a fair-weather wave height of about **0.5 m** (period 6 to 7 s), and reserve **3.2 to 3.5 m** for the June to September monsoon, when the fort is closed ([IMD bulletin](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/uploads/archive/45/45_3491c1_fisherman.pdf)).

## The wall is nominally 9.1 m by 3.66 m, and condition varies more than geometry

Both headline numbers are **nominal, not measured**. The gazetteer gives the height as 29 to 30 ft (8.84 to 9.14 m) and the average thickness as 12 ft (3.66 m); the ASI board, as reported in the thesis, says 9.1 m by 3.7 m ([Maharashtra Gazetteer](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/RATNAGIRI/places_Malvan.html); [older gazetteer text](https://shivshahi.tripod.com/sindgaze.html)). Other sources say about 10 m ([Vishwakosh](https://vishwakosh.marathi.gov.in/25772/); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/sindhudurg-the-martaha-fort-that-cemented-shivaji-maharajs-naval-dominance-9549833/)), 9 m, 4 m (a foundation figure), 3 m or 8 to 10 ft, and no source states the datum. The prototype's 9.144 m and 3.6576 m are the top of the gazetteer range and its exact thickness, so they are defensible as defaults but wrong as constants. Two consequences follow from the unstated datum. First, if 9.1 m is measured from the exterior toe at the sea or the rock shelf, the wall stands only about 5.5 to 7 m above the +2.6 to 4.65 m interior; this is my inference, not a sourced fact, and it would shorten the rampart stairs from the 36 to 46 steps a full 9.1 m rise needs to roughly 24 to 32. Second, the visible freeboard from outside and inside the fort will differ, so define height from the local exterior toe and let the interior floor fall where the terrain puts it.

Evidence for variation is condition, not design. In 1828 the north and east faces were fair, with small fig saplings under 3 m, while the west and south faces were deplorable: the parapet was "not above two feet" (0.61 m), the terreplein was gone and the west wall was undermined; by 1862 the circuit was repaired. A **20 ft (6.1 m) breach** on the west is recorded, a **10 x 8 m** section collapsed and was repaired in 2002, and a **3 m breach** opened at the base of the No. 2 bastion in July 2026 ([Pudhari](https://pudhari.news/maharashtra/kokan/sindhudurg/sindhudurg-fort-wall-damage-archeology-department-neglect-ss88)). Locations are mostly unknown, so I recommend a nominal-intact circuit at the design values with these as a toggleable damage layer, rather than random height noise. Thickness is described only as wider in some places and narrower in others, on a broad foundation with lead poured into the footings and lime pointing on sea-exposed bases; the spread is unsourced, so if variation is wanted, use a labelled placeholder of about +/-0.6 m, wider on W/S faces and bastions. Batter, walkway, parapet and merlon dimensions are undocumented. The only anchor is the anecdote that six people walk abreast, which implies a clear walkway of about 3.0 to 3.5 m.

Bastion counts conflict: 22, 32, 42, 52 and 53 all appear, the older gazetteer says 32 towers 40 to 130 yd (36.6 to 118.9 m) apart, the revised gazetteer says 52 towers and 45 staircases, and Vishwakosh and the CCRT say 42; the thesis says between 42 and 52. Over a 2,989 m circuit the mean spacing is 93 m for 32, 71 m for 42 and 57 m for 52. Treat the count as a parameter, default it to **42 perimeter bastions** with spacing kept inside the 37 to 119 m envelope, and place them first at convex corners of the traced outline because the zigzag plan was built for flanking fire. The bastions are described as semicircular with embrasures and a flat seat on the parapet; their radius is undocumented, so a 4 to 6 m radius is a placeholder (about 1 to 1.5 wall thicknesses), not evidence. The **Nishan (Daryaburuj) bastion is interior** in the sources, west of the palace ruins and holding a rainwater pond; Wikimapia puts it at (22.3, -204.1) and OSM building way 426199498 (23.6 x 10.9 m, centroid (26.4, -205.3)) covers the same spot, so remove it from the perimeter set and model it as a freestanding tower platform there. The sources' "west of the palace ruins" cannot be reconciled with that location, because the palace ruins are low foundations in the W/SW and Nishan lies east of Rani chi Vela; flag this as unresolved.

The gate complex is at the NE corner, hidden behind a large sea-projecting bastion in a **gomukhi** (cow-mouth) arrangement. The entrance faces east with a north-facing gap, and there is a spiked timber door with a wicket, an arched guard chowk, a nagarkhana above, and a domed Hanuman shrine in front facing south ([Maharashtra Tourism](https://maharashtratourism.gov.in/fort/sindhudurg/); [Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/)). The passage is narrow with a gentle slope, not a stair. Door dimensions ("9 ft x 3.5 ft", "4 to 5 people at a time") are anecdotal and mutually inconsistent, so 2.0 to 2.5 m clear width is a compromise. The first ~25 m of OSM track 426199529 runs (256, 143), (254, 146), (243, 146), (237, 138), a north-then-west dog-leg that fits the gomukhi description, so route the gate passage along it. Shivaji's hand and foot niches sit on the rampart to the right of the gate; Wikimapia's "Shivaji Padunka" polygon (168 m2) at (257.1, 197.5), about 55 m north of the gate, is the best location. A **small postern** opens in the S/SW rampart toward Rani chi Vela, and a sea gate is recorded as sealed.

## The interior is a sparse village on one lane, and the footprints are untyped

All 20 OSM footprints (excluding the Padmagad building) are plain `building=yes`, with areas of 17 to 222 m2 and a median of about 105 m2, so every role below is my inference from Wikimapia coincidences and the gazetteer, not a tag. The revised gazetteer records 13 houses in 1862 and today's reports mention about 15 families, so **12 to 15 houses** is the range, not 24 ([Durgbharari](https://durgbharari.in/sindhudurg/); [Inditales](https://inditales.com/sindhudurg-fort-konkan-coast)). Comparing footprints with Wikimapia place centres (computed from the two files) separates the houses from everything else.

Roughly 11 to 14 mapped houses result, plus at least one unmapped one: the **Jadhav home at (-26.2, -47.1)** has no footprint within 50 m. This is also where I depart from one input note, which lists way 426199500 as an east-facing house; it coincides with Wikimapia's Shiva temple to within 1.5 m, so a 5.7 x 4.9 m east-facing shrine is the safer reading.

The **temple** is the largest correction. The revised gazetteer gives **45 x 23 ft (13.7 x 7.0 m)**, built in 1695 under Rajaram, with a later sabhamandap added by Shahu; OSM way 426199502 matches the length (13.9 m) and adds width for a plinth or porch ([OSM way 426199502](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/426199502)). The timber-pillared hall belongs to Shri Dev Rameshwar at Girye, not to Sindhudurg, which is the likely origin of the prototype's colonnade ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Dev_Rameshwar_Temple)). Roof form and entrance direction are undocumented; a tiled gable with an end door on the east or west short wall is the low-confidence default, with the long axis near E-W (5 deg from +X). Wikimapia traces a 34.6 x 17.7 m temple building and a 59.5 x 52.6 m compound around (57.3, 29.6), roughly 2.5 times the gazetteer length, which may reflect the later mandap or a court platform; keep it as an optional annex layer rather than the base footprint. The prototype's **28 x 10 m** has no source, though a 13.7 m core plus an undocumented mandap could reach it. Other shrines are the Maruti shrine at the gate, Jarimari near the main track (Wikimapia (214.1, 111.3), 44 m from the gate-side track), a Mahadev temple with a square stepwell behind or beside the main temple, Bhavani (146.1, -250.7) and Mahapurush; Bhavani and Mahadev have no matching footprint. The three named stepped wells, **Dudh Bav (37.6, -136.3), Dahi Bav (58.9, -134.2) and Sakhar Bav (95.9, -174.3)**, are modelled as square wells of about 2 to 3 m inside low parapets (a general Konkan laterite-well size, not a site measurement); the first two flank the lane junction at (46, -138), 8 and 12 m away. Houses are single-storey with tiled roofs, eaves about 2.5 to 3 m and ridge about 1.0 to 1.5 m above (assumptions). The palace ruins are low foundations in the W/SW; do not model granaries, barracks or magazines. A "sweet water lake" depression of about 250 m2 lies at (155.7, 134.7).

The path network is three OSM tracks totalling 879 m: main track **426199529** (432 m, from the gate (255.7, 143.1) SW to the temple court and then south to (73, -166)), branch **426199527** (213 m, from (46, -141) west-south-west to (-150, -195)) and branch **426199528** (234 m, from (197, -283) to (41, -205)). I recomputed clearances in the model frame and they match the input note: the track passes through building 426199525 in its last ~20 m, and runs only 0.9 m from 426199524, 1.2 m from 426199502 and 1.2 m from 426199519. A 2 to 3 m lane needs its centreline at least 2 to 3 m from any footprint (half-width plus a 1 to 1.5 m clearance), so all four violate that. Fix by ending the track at the vertex near (56, -156) or routing it along the south side of 525, and shift the centreline about 2 m off 502's SE corner and 524's west corner. Gaps between footprints are 1.46 m (515 to 524), 2.92 m (504 to 513), 2.99 m (502 to 524) and 3.94 m (509 to 521). Two corrections to the input note follow. Track 528 ends at (41, -205), at the Nishan bastion, not at Rani chi Vela; the track that approaches Rani chi Vela is **527**, ending at (-150, -195), 27 m from the Wikimapia polygon, so use it as the postern approach. And footprints 516, 518, 520 and 522 are 41 to 70 m from any mapped track, with another seven within 14 to 20 m, so the network is incomplete: generate 1.5 to 2 m footpaths to each door rather than leaving doors open to nothing.

For door placement, use one rule instead of hand-tuning: for each building, face the door toward the nearest path point on a side whose clear distance to every other footprint and the wall is at least 3 m, and put no door and no window on a face within 1.5 m of another building (or any rampart). That reproduces the input note's guidance: houses west of the lane (504, 511, 513) face east, 523 faces west, 515 faces north-west, and the temple takes an end door. Note that the OSM tracks' own error (PGS-sourced traces, roughly 1 to 3 m) is of the same order as the 0.9 to 1.2 m violations, so the clearances are partly digitising noise; the model should still resolve them, as clipping will read as a defect either way. The engineering rules I suggest are uncited practitioner conventions: cut openings through the full wall thickness with a boolean, keep a 1 to 1.5 m building-to-path clearance, offset coplanar surfaces by 1 to 2 cm against z-fighting, keep wall solids closed and at least 0.3 m thick, and snap objects to terrain within a 5 cm contact tolerance.

## Scale, sky, sea and vegetation set the visible field

Use **1.65 m** for the adult male reference (eye height about 1.53 m) and **1.525 m** for the female (eye 1.42 m), from NFHS-3 (165 cm) and NCD-RisC (164.95 cm), with a 1.55 m walking camera and +/-5 cm tolerance ([SAGE NFHS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/156482651103200103); [eLife 2016](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/13410/elife-13410-v2.pdf)). I found no Maharashtra, Konkan or 17th-century figure. Historical units are loose (a hath is about 0.457 m, a gaz 0.686 to 0.914 m), so convert gazetteer feet directly.

Palms are the only large vegetation with real evidence: about **300 to 400 coconut palms** (2012), which is 15 to 21 per hectare against 156 to 177 per hectare in a plantation, so they are sparse and clumped around houses, not a grove ([MalvanCity](https://www.malvancity.com/sindhudurg-fort/); [CPCRI](https://cpcri.gov.in/filemgr/webfs/publication/Coconut_English_No133.pdf)). Model height 8 to 18 m (West Coast Tall 15 to 18 m), trunk diameter 0.25 to 0.35 m, crown diameter 6 to 8 m, centre spacing at least 7.5 m in clusters, trunks outside building footprints plus 1 m and at least 2 m from lane centrelines (the last two are my rules; crowns may overhang roofs). One baobab is recorded in 1880 and fig saplings grew on the N and E walls in 1828; their present size and positions are unknown, and any other species is a model choice. Treat anything beyond coconut and fig as decorative.

The sun path below is calculated for 16.0425 N, 73.459 E (IST) and is not cited; use it to validate a solar-position routine to about 1 degree. At 09:00 and 16:00 the sun stands at 32 to 36 deg (azimuth 101) and 36 to 39 deg (azimuth 256 to 259) at the equinoxes, 39 deg (73) and 42 deg (287) in June, and 25 deg (126) and 26 deg (233) in December. The sun is north of the zenith only from early May to early August. A 9.1 m wall casts a **7.5 m** noon shadow toward +Y in December and a 1 m post casts 0.82 m; in June the shadow is only about 1.2 m, pointing south.

The seascape has a long clear horizon: **11.6 km** from the wall top and 4.6 km from the ground. Malvan is about 2.0 km away at a bearing near 4 deg, Sarjekot 4.6 km near 1 deg, Rajkot fort about 2 km north, Tarkarli about 6 km south, the Vengurla Rocks lighthouse about 8 km south, and the Sahyadri ridge a faint 0.6 deg on the eastern horizon ([Trawell Rajkot](https://www.trawell.in/maharashtra/malvan-sindhudurg/rajkot-fort)). These are calculated from point coordinates and carry medium-to-low confidence. For Padmagad, the OSM building at about (606, 537) and islet centroid near (577, 549) give roughly 0.77 km at 41 deg east of north from the fort's area centre, shorter than the 1.15 km at 53 deg in the visual-field note; prefer the OSM coordinates. The ferry carries 20 passengers plus 2 crew in 15 to 20 minutes, and the OSM ferry terminal node (387.3, 318.8) is about 220 m from the gate.

Modern additions should be a toggleable present-day layer: a Tourist Information Centre just inside the gate, a ticket counter, small shops, an iron grille at the footprint shrine, tiled houses for 10 to 20 households, OSM piers 426199491 and 426199494, and an ASI repair tender for 2024 to 2025 ([Sigma Tenders](https://sigmatenders.com/tender/66g45Eo5PGMS696pckgn7s)). I found nothing on poles, signage or a flagpole. The historic flag at the Nishan bastion was 28 ft (8.5 m) until 1812; the "228 ft" figure in one source is doubtful. Haze data are absent, so no visibility limit is justified.

## The four data files fix position, not form

`osm_sindhudurg.json` (8.7 MB, 620 elements, ODbL) holds the islet coastline (way 19804981), the OSM fort node, the gate node 2713509503, two wall ways (426199526, a `city_wall` of 68.8 m at centroid (60.4, 124.5), and 426199496, 41.5 m at (20.6, -162.8)), the 20 footprints and three tracks above, piers, the ferry node, Padmagad and the rock polygons ([OSM copyright](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)). It carries no wall height, thickness or bastions, and only two wall segments, so the wall trace must come from elsewhere. `unesco_1739-011_sindhudurg_boundary.geojson` has two features: the core (341 vertices, 19.1 ha) and the buffer (70 vertices, 783.7 ha); the licence is unverified ([UNESCO maps](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1739/maps)). `wikimapia_places.json` holds 33 places with `coords_lonlat` polygons (longitude first), pre-computed model-frame `X`, `Y`, `area_m2` and minimum-bounding-rectangle sizes; the licence is CC BY-SA 3.0 ([Wikimapia](https://wikimapia.org/)). `dem_samples.json` holds 53 points across 11 datasets (nearest-neighbour sampling) plus the raw windows, and its `meta` records the projection used. Its X constant is 111,320 x cos(16.0425 deg), about 106,982 m per degree, against 106,996 m used elsewhere; that differs by under 0.1 m across the island and does not matter.

Use them as follows. Take the shoreline from the OSM coastline, the wall centreline from the UNESCO core simplified at about 1 m (any of the three outlines would do; each is a wall-line proxy good to about +/-10 m and none carries bastions or thickness), footprints, angles and tracks from OSM, and named positions (wells, shrines, houses, gate, Rani chi Vela, Nishan) from Wikimapia, cross-checked against OSM as in the table above. Use the DEMs only as a low-frequency check, masked by the coastline. Wall height, thickness, bastion count and profile cannot come from any file. Be aware of the frame offset between inputs: the interior-buildings note works in a gate-origin frame, where model X is gate X + 255.7 and Y is gate Y + 143.1 (the temple at gate (-182, -116) is (73.3, 27.0) in the model frame); convert it before reuse, and prefer the way IDs.

Against the prototype: the 9.144 m constant should become a 9.1 m crest above the local toe with a damage overlay; the 3.6576 m thickness is fine as a mean; the temple, house count and Nishan bastion change as above; the 2.6 to 4.65 m terrain is acceptable but should widen to 2.0 to 5.0 m with a lower W/S foot; and the gate position should move. I did not open the UNESCO dossier PDFs ([UNESCO doc 209714](https://whc.unesco.org/document/209714); [doc 222454](https://whc.unesco.org/document/222454)), which may contain measured drawings, nor view the National Maritime Museum charts of 1765 and 1886 ([RMG 549769](https://rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-549769)), which are Crown copyright. I found no LiDAR, measured survey drawings or drone model.

## Conclusion

The most useful result is a datum problem, not a number: if the nominal 9.1 m is measured from the sea-side toe, the model's rampart, stairs and interior sightlines change materially, because the wall would stand only about 5.5 to 7 m above the interior ground. That makes the exterior toe elevation the single parameter worth getting right before anything else, and the first measurement worth seeking is a drawn section or a drone DSM through one wall and bastion.

The second is that Wikimapia and OSM, read together, classify the otherwise untyped footprints: the Shiva shrine, the Mahapurush shrine, the gate building and the Nishan bastion fall out of 20 plain buildings, leaving 11 to 14 houses and a clear gap in the path network. The risk in this build is false precision, since the position errors (1 to 10 m) are as large as the clearances being enforced; build every placement as a rule with a tolerance, so the dossier PDFs or a survey can replace a value without redoing the scene.
